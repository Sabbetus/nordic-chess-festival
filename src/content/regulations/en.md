## 2 § General regulations

The tournament will be played in accordance with the FIDE Laws of Chess, FIDE Fair Play Regulations, other applicable FIDE regulations and the competition regulations of the Finnish Chess Federation.

The results will be sent to the national rating system (selo) and to the FIDE rating system (elo).

**Players are allowed to take one half-point (½) bye during the first five (5) rounds. The Chief Arbiter must be notified of the use of a bye before the pairings for the round are published.**

Players are not allowed to eat at the chessboard. Non-alcoholic beverages are allowed at the chessboard.

**Players are not allowed to offer or agree to a draw before both players have made at least twenty (20) moves.**

Default time is thirty (30) minutes.

It is possible to achieve FIDE title norms (GM, IM, WGM and WIM) in the tournament.

## 4 § Pairing system

Nine (9) rounds in a single group. Pairings will be made according to the FIDE Swiss system. The Swiss-Manager program will be used for the pairings.

If there are at least 140 participants in the tournament, the pairings will be made according to the FIDE-approved Accelerated System (Baku Acceleration, FIDE Handbook C.04.7).

## 5 § Time control

The time control is 90 minutes for 40 moves + 30 minutes + 30 seconds increment per move, starting from move 1. The move counter is not used.

## 6 § Fair Play policy

Bringing electronic devices and other sources of information as defined in Section 11.3 of the Laws of Chess into the playing area is prohibited. They must be handed in at the designated location before entering the playing area. For example, the following are prohibited:

- Mobile phones, laptops, tablets (and any other similar devices)
- Smartwatches and smart rings
- Headphones and earplugs (the organizer has earplugs available if needed)
- Pens
- Chess literature

**A violation of this regulation will result in a loss of the ongoing game.**

If a player needs to hold or use an electronic device during the game for health reasons, permission must be requested from the Chief Arbiter before the start of the competition.

Players may be scanned before entering the playing area. Players are responsible for their own arrival, and screenings may also be conducted for players who arrive late.

Players who have won or drawn their game may be selected to undergo a post-game check immediately after the game ends.

Games may be subject to statistical screening. Players are not allowed to talk to the general public or any other third party while their game is in progress.

Spectators may be in the playing area during the first ten (10) minutes from the start of the round. Spectators must leave their electronic devices in a location designated by the organizer before entering the playing area.

Photographing is permitted in the playing area for the first ten (10) minutes from the start of the round. This restriction does not apply to photographers who have obtained separate permission from the organizer. Photographing with mobile phones is not permitted.

## 7 § Conduct of the players

Silence must be maintained in the playing area during the round. Players are not permitted to comment on or analyse games in progress. Analysing in the playing area or in its immediate vicinity is prohibited. Analysis shall take place in the designated area.

## 8 § Tiebreaks and regulations on prizes

The order of players that finish with the same number of points shall be determined by the following tiebreaks:

1. Median-Buchholz
2. Buchholz – 1
3. Buchholz, no cut
4. Sonneborn-Berger
5. Direct encounter

In the case of unplayed games, the current FIDE Competition Rules shall be applied for the calculation of tiebreaks.

Prizes will be shared among the players in the same point group. **One player may only receive one cash prize.**

## 9 § Data protection

Due to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, 2016/679) the organizer states the following:

The organizer handles personal data of the players and produces the results of the competition. The results will be published. The organizer uses the Swiss-Manager program and the results will be published online at chess-results.com and on rating calculation sites. The results may also be published on other websites or in the media.

Players may be photographed during the tournament, and the material may be published.

## 10 § Other

Results and pairings are published on Chess-Results.com and at the playing venue.

Some of the games will be broadcast using live boards. There is a 15-minute delay in the broadcast.

Decisions made by the Chief Arbiter may be appealed to the Finnish Committee of Chess Arbiters. The appeal must be submitted by email to the committee's secretary, Mika Hakala (mika.hakala@mikahakala.fi), within 30 minutes of the decision. Additionally, a 100-euro appeal fee must be paid. The appeal fee is paid to the organizer. The appeal fee will be refunded if the appeal is upheld.
